# Bug Bounty Program

Copy page Copy page

How Relay classifies and rewards vulnerability reports.

Copy page Copy page

Relay runs a bug bounty program to make responsible disclosure financially rational for security researchers. Rewards scale with how much damage a bug can cause and how feasible it is to exploit in practice. We run monitoring and circuit breakers across the stack, so the likelihood tiers and modifiers reflect real response capacity — not a hypothetical one.This framework applies across Relay’s protocol and services. Relay Vaults is a separate protocol and is not covered by this program (see [Out of scope](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#out-of-scope)).
## [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#how-payouts-are-calculated)

How payouts are calculated

Every valid report goes through five steps:
1.   Classify **severity** (S1–S4) — how bad is the damage if exploited?
2.   Classify **likelihood** (L1–L3) — how feasible is exploitation in practice?
3.   Read the **base payout** from the matrix.
4.   Apply **modifiers** (×0.5 to ×1.5) from the fixed list.
5.   Round to the nearest $500 (ties round up). **Floor: $500. Cap: $100,000.**

### [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#severity)

Severity

Severity captures what happens if the bug is exploited — damage magnitude and how fast it occurs.

| Tier | Rule of thumb | Definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **S4 — Critical** | Can drain everything immediately | Total fund theft or protocol insolvency with no time to react. |
| **S3 — High** | Large or fast theft, or permanent freeze | Large-scale fund theft or permanent freeze. Requires some setup, but fast once triggered. |
| **S2 — Medium** | Partial or slow damage | Bounded drain (capped percentage, not total) or slow drain with a response window. Temporary freeze. |
| **S1 — Low** | Annoying but not financially dangerous | Griefing, minor economic damage, DoS with no fund loss, or an admin-only bug with no external exploit path. |

Four rules resolve the most common classification calls:
*   **Theft outranks freeze.** Stealing funds is more severe than locking them, even at a larger nominal amount.
*   **Instant outranks slow.** A slow drain gives us time to detect and pause; an instant drain does not.
*   **If damage is capped**, severity is based on the cap — not total TVL.
*   **Admin-only bugs are S1.** Functions behind `onlyOwner` or an equivalent role do not escalate above S1 regardless of the potential damage. They are only eligible for a bounty when there is an external path to reach the role (see below).

### [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#likelihood)

Likelihood

Likelihood captures how probable exploitation is in practice.

| Tier | Rule of thumb | Criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **L3 — High** | Anyone can do it anytime | No prerequisites. Callable by any EOA with publicly available calldata. |
| **L2 — Medium** | Needs setup (flash loans, timing, conditions) | Requires specific conditions: flash loan infra, a particular chain state, MEV infrastructure, or another protocol being in a particular state. |
| **L1 — Low** | Needs a privileged role or rare situation | Requires privileged access (operator, solver, oracle), very specific market conditions, or a multi-step attack depending on several parties. |

We reduce likelihood by one tier (floored at L1) when the exploit freezes rather than steals (unless the freeze is a step toward theft).
## [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#base-payout-matrix)

Base payout matrix

|  | **L3 — High** | **L2 — Medium** | **L1 — Low** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S4 — Critical** | $100,000 | $50,000 | $20,000 |
| **S3 — High** | $30,000 | $15,000 | $7,500 |
| **S2 — Medium** | $10,000 | $5,000 | $2,000 |
| **S1 — Low** | $2,000 | $1,000 | $500 |

The floor of $500 applies only when a report demonstrates an external exploit path — an attacker without a privileged role can reach the bug.A report is not eligible for a bounty when:
*   The only exploit path requires a privileged role (owner, operator, solver, oracle).
*   The reporter does not demonstrate that an unprivileged party can reach that role or induce the privileged party to act.
*   The bug would not fire under normal operation.

These are real bugs and we still want them reported — they just do not qualify as vulnerabilities under this framework. Strong write-ups in this category are candidates for a discretionary bonus.
## [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#modifiers)

Modifiers

After landing on a base payout, apply modifiers multiplicatively. Modifiers are drawn from the fixed list below.**Quick checklist:**
*   ×1.5 — permanently unrecoverable damage
*   ×1.25 — hard to fix / hard to detect
*   ×0.75 — expensive to exploit, or pausable with active monitoring
*   ×0.5 — damage capped to a sub-pool

**Full trigger definitions:**

| Modifier | Trigger | Factor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Permanently unrecoverable | Damage cannot be reversed after exploit — freeze with no pause or upgrade, or theft with funds laundered/bridged before response is possible | ×1.5 |
| High remediation cost | Fix requires a full contract redeploy across multiple chains | ×1.25 |
| Silent / undetectable | No onchain event emitted, no alert surface, could run unnoticed for days | ×1.25 |
| High attacker cost | Exploit requires significant capital at risk or fees that materially reduce net gain | ×0.75 |
| Pausable + monitored | Contract can be paused. Active monitoring with sub-5-minute response is in place and would meaningfully reduce loss against this specific exploit | ×0.75 |
| Bounded damage pool | Damage capped to a sub-pool, not full TVL (e.g. fee buffer only) | ×0.5 |

The **permanently unrecoverable** modifier (×1.5) applies when damage from an exploit cannot be reversed once it happens. This is different from how quickly the damage occurs (which is captured by severity tiers). This modifier is used if the funds or system state cannot be restored after the exploit, such as when funds are stolen and removed before any response is possible (e.g., laundered or bridged out), or when a freeze is permanent with no way to pause or upgrade the contract.

Modifiers stack. The final amount is floored at $500, capped at $100,000, and rounded to the nearest $500.**Worked example.** S3/L2 base = $15,000. Pausable (×0.75) and bounded to the fee buffer (×0.5) → $15,000 × 0.75 × 0.5 = $5,625 → **$5,500**.
## [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#worked-examples)

Worked examples

### [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#flash-loan-reentrancy-on-withdrawal)

Flash loan reentrancy on withdrawal

An attacker uses a flash loan to reenter the withdrawal function and drain depository contract funds in a single transaction. No special permissions required.
*   **Severity: S4** — instant, total fund theft.
*   **Likelihood: L2** — requires flash loan infrastructure and a specific reentrancy window.
*   **Base: $50,000.** Permanently unrecoverable (×1.5) — funds laundered in the same tx before any response is possible. **Final: $75,000.**

### [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#fee-parameter-drift)

Fee parameter drift

A rounding error lets any filler extract ~2 bps above their entitled fee per fill. Callable by any EOA, damage accumulates slowly, bounded to the fee buffer.
*   **Severity: S2** — slow drain, bounded to the fee pool.
*   **Likelihood: L3** — any EOA, any time.
*   **Base: $10,000.** Bounded damage pool (×0.5) and pausable + monitored (×0.75) → $3,750 → **$4,000**.

### [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#permanent-pool-freeze-via-griefing)

Permanent pool freeze via griefing

A crafted cross-chain message bricks a specific pool’s accounting, locking all funds in it. The attacker has no way to extract — it is a pure grief and costs gas.
*   **Severity: S3** — permanent freeze of a pool.
*   **Likelihood: L2** — downgraded one tier from L3 because the exploit freezes rather than steals.
*   **Base: $15,000.** Permanently unrecoverable (×1.5) → **$22,500**.

## [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#edge-cases)

Edge cases

*   **“The fix is already deployed.”** Payouts are assessed as of report submission, not review. A valid report does not lose value because we moved fast.
*   **“The bug only exists under specific token configurations.”** If the configuration is live in production, the report is treated as real. If only theoretical, likelihood drops to L1.
*   **“The reporter also proposed the fix.”** Does not change the tier, but is strong grounds for a discretionary bonus.
*   **Duplicate reports.** The first submission gets the full payout. Duplicate reports are not eligible for an automatic reward, regardless of when they are submitted. We may award a discretionary bonus when a duplicate adds material new evidence, such as another affected chain, a PoC that changes the assessment, or a useful fix proposal. This change applies to reports submitted on or after the publication of this update. Reports submitted earlier remain subject to the terms in effect when they were submitted.
*   **Previously known issues.** We treat a finding as already known only when, before your submission, the same root cause was recorded in a dated internal record showing we were already tracking it for remediation, such as an internal ticket or a triaged finding. Raw automated scanner output or an untriaged internal note does not count. When a qualifying record predates your report, the report is not eligible for an automatic reward. We may award a discretionary bonus when the report adds material new evidence. We will not reduce a payout based on internal discovery we cannot date to before your submission, and a report never loses value because we detected, triaged, or fixed the issue after you submitted it. This change applies to reports submitted on or after the publication of this update. Reports submitted earlier remain subject to the terms in effect when they were submitted.

## [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#discretionary-bonuses)

Discretionary bonuses

Separate from the matrix, we may award bonuses for:
*   Exceptional write-ups with clear root cause and a suggested fix.
*   Novel attack vectors that reveal a systemic pattern.
*   Proactive disclosure during an active incident.
*   Material new evidence in a duplicate or previously known report.

Bonuses are recognition, not entitlement, and are not pre-committed.
## [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#poc-requirements)

PoC requirements

| Severity | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **S4, S3** | Runnable PoC required. Explanations without code are not accepted. |
| **S2** | PoC strongly preferred. A detailed technical explanation is accepted when a PoC is not feasible. |
| **S1** | Description sufficient with clear reproduction steps. |

## [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#out-of-scope)

Out of scope

The following are not eligible for a reward:
*   Attacks the reporter has already exploited.
*   Issues requiring phishing, social engineering, or a compromised private key.
*   Findings in third-party protocols we compose with, unless the root cause is in Relay’s code.
*   DoS with no fund loss and no path to fund loss.
*   Theoretical issues with no viable PoC against in-scope assets.
*   Relay Vaults. The Vaults contracts (RelayPool, RelayBridge, RelayPoolFactory, RelayBridgeFactory, RelayPoolNativeGateway), the vaults API and the vaults user interface are not in-scope assets. Reports against Relay Vaults are not eligible for a reward.

## [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#reward-process-and-requirements)

Reward process and requirements

*   **Identity and business verification.** Individuals must complete Relay’s KYC and background check. Business entities must complete Relay’s KYB. No reward is paid until verification is complete.
*   **Direct payment.** Relay pays rewards only to the verified individual or business that submitted the report. Third-party payment arrangements are not supported. This requirement also applies to unresolved third-party payment requests for reports submitted before the publication of this update. Payment for those reports must go directly to the verified reporting individual or business.
*   **Verification and tax documents.** We request verification and tax documents, such as a W-8BEN, W-9, or the equivalent for your jurisdiction, separately after reward approval. Do not include them with the initial report.
*   **Program participation.** Relay may suspend or exclude any participant from the bounty program at any time, for any reason, at Relay’s discretion. Suspension or exclusion affects future participation only. Reports submitted before suspension or exclusion continue to be assessed under the applicable program terms. Suspension or exclusion alone does not disqualify those reports or cancel approved rewards.
*   **Payment.** Rewards are paid in cryptocurrency.

## [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#reporting-a-vulnerability)

Reporting a vulnerability

Email [support@relay.link](mailto:support@relay.link). Send one email for each vulnerability. Do not combine several findings in one report.Use a clear subject line that names the affected component and the issue. Example: `Vulnerability report: Approval Proxy signature replay`.
### [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#what-to-include)

What to include

Put all relevant information in the first email. Include:
*   A description of the vulnerability and the affected contract, service, or endpoint.
*   The affected chain, deployment, or version.
*   Step-by-step reproduction instructions.
*   The demonstrated impact and exploit conditions, where applicable.
*   A runnable PoC where the severity requires one. See [PoC requirements](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#poc-requirements).
*   Supporting material: transaction hashes, addresses, logs, or screenshots.

A report without the required material is not complete. Review starts when we receive the complete report.
### [​](https://docs.relay.link/security/bounties#what-happens-next)

What happens next

*   We aim to acknowledge reports within 2 business days. An acknowledgment confirms receipt only. It does not confirm validity, severity, reward eligibility, or payment.
*   We aim to send an initial review update within 7 calendar days of receiving a complete report. Full assessment and remediation can take longer.
*   You may add new evidence at any time by replying in the original email thread.
*   Wait until the 7-day review window ends before asking for a status update.
*   Do not send a second report or open a new ticket for the same finding. Duplicate tickets slow the review.
*   A different vulnerability needs a new email.
*   We may explain the basis for our assessment, but we will not share other researchers’ reports, private tracking records, or confidential security details.
